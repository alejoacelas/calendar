# Research method — 2026-07-26

## Question

Which finished systems or composable tools can maintain an evidence-backed model of future availability and answer questions such as “am I too busy in the first week of September?”

## Method

Four parallel tracks covered finished products, calendar infrastructure and UI, AI/data ingestion, and the state/forecast design. A separate review challenged access gates, arithmetic, calibration, security, and whether the trials could falsify the recommendation. Claims were checked against official product help, API references, repositories, security pages, and current pricing pages. Secondary roundups were discovery aids only. A tool remained a candidate only if it either handled a major module through a supported API, CLI, MCP, or export, or attempted the full job.

The comparison separates:

- promised AI from documented inputs and outputs;
- calendar occupancy from total workload;
- current retrieval from durable state;
- supported developer surfaces from reverse-engineered endpoints;
- historical analytics from forward capacity;
- a scheduling horizon from an arbitrary query horizon.

No accounts were connected and no personal calendar, mail, or documents were read. The locally installed `gog v0.31.1` and its checked-out documentation were inspected without reading account data.

## Limits

This is source and API research, not an authenticated product benchmark. Current claims describe documented capability; [the trial protocol](../docs/05-trials.md) is designed to measure actual retrieval completeness, arithmetic, latency, deletion, and model consistency before purchase or implementation.
